# Styles

Pick the look that matches the brand's real tone. State why in one sentence.

| Style | Looks like | Fits brands that feel |
|---|---|---|
| Pencil / crayon storybook | Colored-pencil texture, wobbly outlines, muted palette, paper | Gentle, cozy, family, nostalgic, handmade |
| Ink on paper | Pen line and hatching on warm paper | Thoughtful, literary, heritage, craft, editorial |
| Screen print / "case file" | Flat bold shapes, limited inks, halftone dots, misregistration | Urgent, bold, investigative, news, activist, streetwear |
| Risograph | Fluorescent inks, grainy overprints, playful misalignment | Youthful, creative, indie, music, food and drink |
| Graphite minimal | Sparse pencil, lots of space, torn-paper sections | Calm, premium, wellness, architecture, luxury |
| Flat geometric vector-print | Clean shapes with grain added, confident color blocks | Modern, tech, finance, B2B, clear and trustworthy |
| Paper cutout collage | Layered cut paper, shadows between layers, mixed textures | Warm, community, education, nonprofits, local business |

Colors always come from the brand brief, not the style. If a style's usual palette clashes with the brand, keep the texture and technique and swap in the brand colors.

Describe the chosen style in the prompts with concrete rendering terms (outlines, textures, shading, background). Never just give the style's name.

Tone for character proportions:
- Playful brands get chunky, cute proportions and big dot eyes.
- Serious or premium brands get balanced proportions and calmer expressions. Charm comes from motion, not cuteness.
- Regulated or somber topics (health, legal, grief) use warmth, not jokes. Villains, if any, stay cartoonish, not scary.
