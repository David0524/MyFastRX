# Plan: three UGC videos (Oct 2026). Nothing is generated until this is approved.

Sources: CLIENT_BRIEF_GLP1_2026-10.md (guidance) + HANDOFF.md decisions, UGC_PLAYBOOK.md, HANDOVER.md section 2.

## The situation
- **Category:** compounded GLP-1 telehealth is crowded and price-led, and buyers are wary: of hidden fees and
  auto-ship, of whether compounded care is legitimate, of whether they'll even qualify. The ads that convert answer one
  of those worries in the first seconds, then make the next step feel small.
- **Buyer:** women 35-64, self-pay, comparing options on their phone. Calm, plain talk from someone like them beats
  hype; polish reads as an ad and gets skipped.
- **Our edge (approved wording):** "Compounded Semaglutide starting at $69" / "Compounded Tirzepatide starting at $129",
  provider review + medication + shipping included, no subscription or membership fees, same price at any
  provider-approved dose, U.S.-licensed providers, dispensed by U.S. pharmacies.

## The three videos: one worry each, three stages of the funnel
| | Video | Main idea | Who it's for | Why it should convert |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **"What $69 actually covers"** (+ a $129 Tirzepatide twin) | The intro price and what's in it | Ready-to-buy shoppers comparing prices | Price is the #1 search driver; showing what's included pre-empts the "hidden fees" worry |
| 2 | **"What's the catch?"** | Trust: no subscription, real providers, U.S. pharmacies | Skeptics who have seen too many GLP-1 ads | Names the doubt out loud, which earns attention, then answers it with approved facts |
| 3 | **"Three steps"** | How easy it is to start from home | First-timers unsure what happens next | Makes the click feel small: intake, provider review, delivery if prescribed |

Why not exactly the brief's three (Sema offer, Tirz offer, three steps): the Sema and Tirz offers are the same video
with one line changed, so we make the Tirz version as a cheap **twin** of video 1 (same takes except the price line)
and use the third slot for the trust video, which no current ad covers. That still delivers all three of the brief's
starting concepts, plus one.

## One host for all three
- New host: Black or Hispanic woman, mid-40s, shoulder-length dark brown hair, average or mid-size build, relatable.
- Recurring face so the feed builds familiarity; a second host comes later for A/B tests (re-cast a winner).
- Creator profile: a regular woman filming on her phone. Plain, slightly awkward cutaways (phone propped on the
  counter), not aesthetic B-roll.
- Voice: the video model's own voice from her first approved take, then a **locked prompt** (only the words change), so
  she sounds the same in every ad.
- She is always a presenter: no "I'm on it", no results, no body talk.

## Every video
- Captions word for word, in the safe zone; the price line always says "Compounded" with "Introductory offer" beside $69 / $129.
- End card (2.5 s): logo, "Check if I qualify", MyFastRx.com, the small approved vial (semaglutide videos only), BBB badge,
  the brief's intro-offer disclaimer + "Prescription required. Subject to provider approval."
- Subtle licensed music only under the end card (or none); her voice carries the ad.
- Two hooks per video (spoken vs. a text-only first 2 s): an edit, not a reshoot, so 4 videos become 8 ads.
- Deliver 9:16 MP4 + editable source; 4:5 feed versions re-laid out when requested.

## Testing (Meta first)
- Launch all 8 ads with even budgets to /weight-loss/special/.
- Read after 5-7 days: hook rate (3-second views), hold rate, click-through, cost per started visit.
- Then: shift budget to the winners, re-cast the best one with a second host, write new hooks for the winning video.
