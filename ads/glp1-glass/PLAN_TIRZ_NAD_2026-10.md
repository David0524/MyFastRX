## 1. The buyer, and what that means for these ads

**Tirzepatide buyer (women 35-64):** has already researched GLP-1s, often knows tirzepatide is the newer and stronger
one, and has seen the prices. She is not short of information; she is short of trust. Her fears, in order:
1. **Price creep and bait-and-switch.** Intro prices that jump, prices that rise with the dose, surprise auto-billing.
2. **Paying and then being turned down.** Especially when checkout comes before the provider review.
3. **"Is compounded legit?"** Who prescribes, who makes it, will it actually arrive.
4. **Hassle and judgement.** Office visits, forms, being asked about her weight.

So the winning tone is "nothing hidden": exact numbers, exact next steps, the catch stated before she asks. Being
open about the $199 regular price helps more than it hurts with this buyer, because hiding it is what she expects.

**NAD+ buyer (broader: 35-60, men and women, wellness and longevity minded):** already sold on NAD+ (IV lounges,
podcasts, friends); our job is not "why NAD+" (we can't make benefit claims anyway) but "where, how, how much, is it
legit". Her objections: needles, the clinic trip and the 1-2 hour drip, the price of IV sessions, and whether an
online prescription is real. A nasal spray is genuine news to this buyer: the curiosity hook in Video 3 is the
strongest idea in the set.

**Platform note (all four):** Meta rejects ads that assert or imply the viewer's medical condition or treatment
("personal attributes"). "Your tirzepatide dose goes up" tells the viewer she is on tirzepatide; "Considering
tirzepatide?" is borderline. The rewrites below talk about tirzepatide in general or in the host's voice.

## 2. Changes to the concepts, and why

### Across all four
- **Code MY70 vs auto-applied (blocker).** The live page says "$70 SAVINGS AUTO-APPLIED" and the Oct 7 brief says
  "Do not show MY70 or tell viewers to enter a code". If checkout now needs the code, fine; if not, drop it. A code is
  friction (one more thing to remember) and leaks to coupon sites.
- **Host stays a presenter, never a patient** (brief section 7, playbook): no unboxing her own order, no "my dose", no
  using the product. Delivery shows a parcel on a doorstep that she doesn't claim.
- **Real product only.** The brief bans invented labels and needs approval for AI medication imagery; Video 3/4 call
  for real product footage. We need product photos (ideally 10-20 s of phone video of a hand holding each product).
- **Spoken spellings for the video model:** "tir-ZEP-a-tide" (test it; fallback "tirz"), "N-A-D plus", "My Fast Rx".
- **Two hooks per video** (the plan's testing order: hook first, then host, then format).
- **9:16 masters, 4:5 versions re-laid-out** (as the four finals), no generation cost for 4:5.

### Video 1: "Higher dose. Same price." (Tirzepatide, 20-25 s)
- **Hook rewrite (Meta-safe):**
  - A: "Okay so tirzepatide doses usually go up over time... at My Fast Rx, the price doesn't."
  - B: "The tirzepatide price thing nobody really explains." (curiosity; Video 4's best-performing shape)
  - On screen from frame 1: **HIGHER DOSE. SAME PRICE.**
- **The graphic must not show $129 as the flat line** (the brief: never imply intro prices continue). Dose steps
  1 → 4 rise (no mg numbers, label "Dose set by your provider"); the flat line reads "Your plan price". No bar graph
  (client feedback on v7's dose proof): a stepped line plus a price chip that stays locked.
- **Say the catch first:** "$129 first month. Then $199 a month, at any dose." This is accurate (1-month plan regular
  price), it is the whole point of the concept, and it turns "intro price" suspicion into trust. Needs client OK.
- **"No automatic refill billing" needs confirming.** The GLP-1 page says "cancel at any time before your next refill"
  and the refund page says "cancel your subscription". We can't say it unless it's true for tirzepatide plans.
- Closing: "Compounded tirzepatide starts at a hundred and twenty-nine DOLLARS, first month. Check if you qualify,
  takes like a minute." End card: Compounded Tirzepatide / $129 first month / Introductory offer / Same price at any
  dose within your plan / disclaimer (brief section 9, intro version) + "Prescription required. Subject to provider
  approval."

### Video 2: "Tirzepatide online, step by step" (25-30 s)
- **Hook:** "Here's exactly how getting tirzepatide online works." (drops the "Considering...?" question).
- **Checkout before review is the scariest step:** pair it with what happens if she isn't prescribed. The refund page
  only covers "orders canceled before shipment". If a not-prescribed order is fully refunded, say so on screen ("Not
  prescribed? Full refund."); it's the strongest line available for this buyer. If not, say nothing about refunds.
- **Real screens with sample data:** we won't type fake people into the live checkout (it creates records, SMS
  opt-ins, maybe charges). Need a test/staging link or the client's own screen recording with sample data, showing:
  state + name → plan selection → checkout ($129 shown) → a few intake questions.
- **Three steps, one literal visual each**, numbered chips 1/2/3; Dana returns every 2-4 s (house rules); the doorstep
  parcel is the location change (playbook section 4).
- Add "Free express shipping" only as written on the site; never "2-day" without the "after pharmacy fulfillment"
  caveat (brief section 6).

### Video 3: "Wait... NAD+ comes as a nasal spray?" (20 s)
- Keep the hook: it's the best one. On screen: **NAD+ WITHOUT NEEDLES?**
- **Holding the product:** the old UGC guardrail says nobody holds the medication; this concept needs her to. Proposal:
  she holds the sealed spray (never opens or uses it), with the real label composited from the client's product photo
  (tracked like the phone in v7). Needs client OK.
- **Replace the unboxing** (implies she's the patient) with a hands-only kit flat-lay, no face, **no syringes in
  frame** (the kit includes insulin syringes), or the doorstep parcel.
- **Use the landing page's own words for the CTA:** the NAD+ checkout opens on "FREE INITIAL SCREENING · About 60
  seconds · No obligation · Check My Eligibility". "Free 60-second screening" lowers the commitment and matches the
  page (message match). The client's "Start Your Online Visit" also works; A/B it.
- Offer from 12 s to the end (4 s+ before the 3 s end card): "From $99/mo" with "3-month plan · $297 upfront"
  directly beneath.
- **Any "why":** only with client-approved wording (the site's "Support your energy and focus from home" is a
  candidate). Otherwise no benefit words at all.

### Video 4: "The NAD+ appointment? Skip the trip." (20 s)
- Product in frame at 0 s (the brief: product in the first second): the spray and vial already on the counter, she sets
  her keys down beside them.
- Hook: "NAD+... without leaving the house? Here's how." On screen: **NAD+ CARE. FROM HOME.**
- Options beat: vial and spray side by side. No syringe, no administration.
- **Optional hook B (needs counsel):** the site itself says IV sessions run "$550-$1,000+ per session". "NAD+ IV
  sessions can run $550 or more. This starts at $99 a month." is the strongest price anchor for IV-aware buyers,
  and it's a category comparison, not a competitor, but it's a comparative claim.
- Host: test a second host here (the UGC plan's Host B; a man 45-55 would tell us if NAD+ skews male). Needs client
  OK: the casting rule says every host is a Black or Hispanic woman 45-55, and that may be GLP-1 only.

## 3. Questions for the client (blocking)
1. **MY70:** must viewers enter it now, or is $70 still auto-applied? (Brief says don't show the code.)
2. **Recurring billing on tirzepatide plans:** can we say "no automatic refill billing"? (GLP-1 page and refund page
   suggest refills/"subscription".)
3. **Regular price on screen:** OK to show "$129 first month, then $199/mo at any dose"?
4. **Not prescribed = refund?** If yes, exact wording we can use.
5. **Screens:** a test/staging checkout or a screen recording with sample data (Video 2, 3, 4).
6. **Product assets:** real photos/phone video of the NAD+ nasal spray, NAD+ vial, kit and shipping box; a
   Tirzepatide vial image (none approved yet; or Video 1-2 end cards run without a vial).
7. **Host holding the sealed product** (Video 3, 4): OK?
8. **NAD+ host:** Dana, or a second host (a man 45-55 to test)?
9. **NAD+ wording:** any approved benefit line; the IV price comparison; the NAD+ disclaimer (client line +
   brief's multi-month version, since $99/mo is a 3-month equivalent).

**Also worth passing on (not blocking):**
- Louisiana isn't in the checkout's state list: exclude it from ad targeting (or confirm).
- Use clean landing URLs with our own UTMs: the NAD+ link in the brief carries `utm_source=chatgpt.com`.
- The NAD+ page has claims ("Cognitive benefits often appear within the first week") and named patient
  testimonials; Meta reviews landing pages, so counsel should look before spend.
- The refund page says "cancel your subscription", which contradicts the ads' "no subscription".
